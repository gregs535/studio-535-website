How to Organize a Maker Business With Notion: A Simple System

Published by Studio 535 · Precision Crafted. Technology Engineered.

Maker businesses rarely fail for lack of talent. They fail because the owner keeps everything in their head — the order statuses, the client who wants a follow-up, the material that's almost gone, the post that's supposed to go out Thursday. That head-keeping is a tax. It's the difference between a business you run and a hobby that runs you.

Notion is the best free tool for fixing this, because it's one workspace that can hold everything. The trap is over-building: fifty databases nobody updates. Here's the minimum system that actually survives contact with a busy week — five databases and three views.

The Five Databases

1. Orders

Every order gets a row. Properties: status (Draft, In Production, Shipped, Complete), customer link, deadline, price, source (Etsy, custom, wholesale). This is your single source of truth — the one question every owner asks ("where is this order?") is now a one-click filter.

2. Clients

Name, contact, last contacted, notes. Link every order to its client. Now "what have I done for this person" and "who should I email next week" answer themselves.

3. Inventory

Material or product, quantity on hand, reorder point, supplier. One low-stock view tells you what to buy this week instead of discovering it at the worst moment.

4. Content Calendar

Every post, product launch, and email. Date, platform, status. The goal isn't a fancy calendar — it's that nothing lives only in your head.

5. Projects

Bigger-than-one-order work: a new product line, a rebrand, a wholesale push. Link orders, content, and clients to it so context isn't scattered.

The Three Views That Do the Work

  • Today view: Orders due this week + inventory below reorder + content due today. Everything that needs you, in one place, every morning.
  • Follow-up view: Clients not contacted in 14 days. The revenue you'd otherwise forget.
  • Quarterly view: Projects grouped by status. Proof the business is moving, not just busy.

Setup Rules (Keep It Alive)

Three rules keep the system from rotting: (1) the Today view is the only thing you check daily — everything else is weekly; (2) if a field isn't useful, delete it — no future-proofing; (3) batch the update. Five minutes in the morning, five at night. A system you maintain daily beats a perfect one you abandon.

You don't need more discipline. You need the mental load somewhere it can't be lost.
