How to Run AI on Your Own Computer — No Cloud, No Subscription

August 12, 2026 · Studio 535

Every week a new AI subscription shows up in someone's budget: $20 here, $20 there, and suddenly you're paying $100+ a month for tools that phone home with everything you type. There's a different way: run the model on your own computer.

This isn't a science project. A mid-range GPU — or even a decent laptop — can run genuinely useful AI that:

  • Never sends your prompts anywhere
  • Works offline
  • Costs $0 per query after setup
  • Has no usage limits, no "continue" paywalls, no guardrails you don't choose

What you actually need

The honest hardware table (Q4 quantization, the sweet spot):

  • 3B model (~2 GB VRAM) — classification, tagging, light chat. Any 8 GB laptop.
  • 7–9B model (~6 GB) — general chat, writing, code. An 8 GB GPU or 16 GB Mac.
  • 14B (~10 GB) — better reasoning, long writing. 12–16 GB GPU.
  • 27B (~17 GB) — strong reasoning, unrestricted writing. 24 GB GPU (used 3090s go for ~$600).

No GPU? A 7B model on a modern CPU still runs at usable speed (~10–20 tokens/sec). Fine for chat and writing.

The 15-minute install

Ollama is the easiest path. Two commands:

curl -fsSL https://ollama.com/install.sh | sh
ollama pull gemma4:latest

Verify it's alive and actually responding:

curl -s http://localhost:11434/api/tags

Why this matters right now

Cloud AI is convenient, but it's a subscription you rent forever. Local AI is an asset you own. For a few hundred dollars of used hardware, you get private, unlimited, offline intelligence — and you stop paying rent on a tool that reads everything you type.
